# Camping Nigeria Growth and Market Opportunity Research

**Working strategy for becoming Nigeria's premier camping rental, supply and distribution company before building software for the industry**  
**Date:** September 6, 2026  
**Status:** Strategic market and operating plan, not a final capital-allocation decision

## Executive conclusion

Camping Nigeria should not become a broad outdoor retailer that happens to run camps.

It should become the **infrastructure company for camping and organized outdoor activity in Nigeria**.

The strongest long-term model is a four-part business:

1. **Experiences and programmes** create demand and trust.
2. **Rental** makes quality equipment accessible without forcing customers to own it.
3. **Retail and distribution** serve customers who want to buy and create supplier scale.
4. **Operating infrastructure** standardizes inventory, maintenance, logistics, safety and delivery, eventually becoming the software product.

The current business already has the right starting point. Camping Nigeria publicly positions itself around structured outdoor programmes for schools, organizations and individuals, Duke of Edinburgh expedition support, and premium gear rental.[1][2] Its current offer architecture includes school programmes, company field days and leadership expeditions, open camps and private camps.[1][3]

The next stage should not be "add more activities."

The next stage should be:

> **Own more of the camping value chain.**

That means becoming the company a customer can call for any of the following:

- rent camping gear;
- buy camping gear;
- equip a school expedition;
- equip a corporate retreat;
- furnish an outdoor event or temporary camp;
- source a hard-to-find outdoor product;
- build a camping programme;
- set up and strike a complete campsite;
- maintain and repair gear;
- procure equipment in bulk;
- build a permanent or semi-permanent campsite;
- train staff on equipment use and camp operations.

The market is still relatively underdeveloped. Nigeria imported approximately **US$317,000 of synthetic-fibre tents in 2024**, with China supplying about 93% of the reported value.[4] Nigeria's reported sleeping-bag imports were only about **US$25,000 and 1,216 units in 2024**, almost entirely from China.[5] These trade categories do not capture the whole outdoor-equipment market, but they support the view that specialist camping retail and distribution remain relatively shallow.

At the same time, Nigerian online marketplaces already contain thousands of products classified under camping and hiking. Jumia currently shows more than 5,000 listings across tents, lighting, furniture, sleeping equipment, hydration, tools and other categories.[6] This means the opportunity is not simply "put camping products online." The opportunity is **curation, trust, availability, specification, service and institutional supply**.

My recommended strategic position is:

> **Camping Nigeria becomes the trusted outdoor-equipment and camp-operations platform for Nigeria, starting with rental and institutional supply, then building selective retail and wholesale distribution.**

The company should prioritize seven growth lanes:

1. **Rental fleet expansion and standardized gear bundles**
2. **School and youth outdoor-equipment supply**
3. **Institutional and commercial camp infrastructure**
4. **Curated retail of high-frequency outdoor essentials**
5. **Distribution and dealer relationships for selected brands or private-label products**
6. **Repairs, maintenance, cleaning and lifecycle services**
7. **Camp setup, site infrastructure and managed outdoor operations**

The most important commercial insight is that Camping Nigeria does not need to choose between rental and sales.

The same inventory intelligence should determine what gets:

- rented;
- sold;
- bundled;
- reordered;
- repaired;
- retired;
- liquidated;
- private-labelled.

That operating complexity is also exactly what can later produce the software business.

The software should not begin as a separate idea.

It should emerge from the system Camping Nigeria is forced to build to operate better than everyone else.

# 4. Market signal: the equipment market exists, but is fragmented

There is no clean public figure for the complete Nigerian camping-equipment market.

The available data should be treated directionally.

## Tents

World Bank WITS / UN Comtrade reports that Nigeria imported approximately:

- **US$317,440** of synthetic-fibre tents in 2024;
- approximately **113,727 kg**;
- China supplied about **US$295,930**, or roughly 93% of the value.[4]

This does not include every shelter or tent type and may include products used outside recreational camping.

Still, it establishes China as the dominant foreign manufacturing source.

## Sleeping bags

Nigeria reported:

- approximately **US$25,390** of sleeping-bag imports in 2024;
- approximately **1,216 units**;
- roughly 99% of the reported value came from China.[5]

This is extremely small compared with mature outdoor markets.

It suggests one or more of the following:

- limited consumer penetration;
- classification differences;
- informal import channels;
- low specialist retail development;
- customers substituting ordinary bedding.

All of those create opportunity for a business that develops the category.

## Online supply

Jumia lists more than **5,000 products** in its camping and hiking category, including:

- tents and shelters;
- sleeping bags;
- bedding;
- lights;
- backpacks;
- furniture;
- footwear;

Jiji also has hundreds of outdoor-gear listings.[7]

The online market therefore appears:

Camping Nigeria should not compete by listing the most SKUs.

# 9. Rental pricing model

Do not price rental items only by competitor rate.

Each asset needs its own lifetime economics.

## Track per asset

- landed purchase cost;
- date acquired;
- useful rental life;
- number of rental days;
- revenue generated;
- cleaning cost;
- repair cost;
- replacement parts;
- transport allocation;
- loss/damage;
- residual resale value.

## Key metric

**Revenue-to-acquisition-cost ratio**

A tent that costs ₦100,000 and produces ₦250,000 of rental revenue before retirement is fundamentally different from one that produces ₦80,000.

## Target rule

Every core rental item should have a defined target number of paid turns required to recover its acquisition cost.

Example:

If a product costs ₦80,000 landed and rents for ₦12,000 per booking:

- 7 gross rentals produce ₦84,000;
- but this is not payback because delivery, cleaning, repair and overhead remain.

The internal model should calculate:

> **true contribution payback**, not simple rental revenue.

### Camping Nigeria Tested: Family

### Camping Nigeria Tested: Expedition

Designed for portability and performance.

The customer does not need to understand 100 product listings.

# 23. Nigeria-specific tent design

Camping Nigeria should not simply copy European camping specifications.

Nigeria creates different requirements.

## Heat

Ventilation matters.

Prioritize:

- large mesh panels;
- cross ventilation;
- shaded vestibules;
- fly designs that reduce heat buildup.

## Rain

Heavy rainfall makes:

- seam sealing;
- hydrostatic resistance;
- bathtub floor;
- drainage;
- rainfly coverage

important.

## Dust

Products should be:

- easy to wipe;
- easy to clean;
- tolerant of repeated deployment.

## Institutional use

School tents need:

- simple setup;
- fewer loose parts;
- durable poles;
- standardized components;
- colour coding;
- replaceable pieces.

## Rental

Products need to survive:

- inexperienced customers;
- repeated folding;
- wet returns;
- transport;
- rough handling.

The best rental tent may be heavier than the best personal backpacking tent.

# 41. Import and regulatory considerations

Camping Nigeria should not assume camping gear is operationally simple to import.

SON states that SONCAP is a pre-shipment conformity programme for imported regulated goods. Commercial products for resale generally need the applicable Product Certificate and SONCAP Certificate route unless specifically exempted.[10][11]

## Before each new category

Confirm:

- HS code;
- import duty;
- VAT;
- applicable Nigerian standard;
- SONCAP status;
- labeling;
- electrical certification where relevant;
- battery transport requirements where relevant.

## Product-specific complexity

### Textiles/tents

Focus on:

- material safety;
- labeling;
- conformity requirements.

### Electrical lighting

Additional electrical product standards may apply.

### Power banks / power stations

Lithium battery transport and electrical conformity introduce additional documentation.

### Cooking equipment

Stoves and fuel systems require greater product-safety diligence.

### First-aid products

Medical products may fall under different regulatory regimes depending on contents.

Do not import mixed containers with dozens of categories before a broker and SONCAP conformity partner map each product.

## Sales inventory

Products leave permanently.

- headlamps;
- bags;

# 43. QR-code every meaningful rental asset

Do this before the company becomes larger.

A QR or barcode should allow staff to see:

- item ID;
- model;
- purchase date;
- purchase cost;
- current condition;
- last rental;
- current booking;
- missing accessories;
- repair history;
- cleaning history;
- retirement date.

Global rental software such as Booqable and Rentman already treats individual asset IDs, barcodes/QR codes, live availability, bundles, check-in/out, repairs and equipment history as core rental workflows.[12][13][14]

Camping Nigeria should build these disciplines internally now, regardless of which software it uses.

# 44. Kits are more important than individual products

A camping operation rarely sends one asset.

It sends a **kit**.

Example:

## 4-Person Tent Kit

- tent body;
- rainfly;
- 2 poles;
- pole repair sleeve;
- 8 stakes;
- 4 guy lines;
- footprint;
- carry bag.

If one stake is missing, the tent may still technically exist but the kit is incomplete.

The internal system should therefore track:

- parent kit;
- required components;
- optional components;
- missing component;
- replacement cost.

This is one of the workflows that generic retail inventory systems often handle poorly.

# 45. Maintenance state

Every rental item should always be in one state.

Example:

- available;
- reserved;
- out;
- returned-unchecked;
- dirty;
- drying;
- inspection;
- repair;
- quarantined;
- retired.

Do not return gear to "available" immediately because the customer returned it.

That is how damaged or wet gear gets sent to the next customer.

## Risk 2: too much inventory

**Critical.**

Outdoor catalogues are enormous.

### Mitigation

Start with proven high-use SKUs and special-order the long tail.

## Risk 3: low utilization

**High.**

Rental equipment makes money only when deployed.

### Mitigation

Use the same fleet across schools, individuals, organizations and B2B rental.

## Risk 4: poor-quality imports

**High.**

A cheap tent can be expensive after repeated failures.

### Mitigation

Field-test samples and track lifetime cost.

## Risk 5: logistics complexity

**High.**

Rental is a logistics business disguised as a product business.

### Mitigation

Track setup, collection, routes and crew as separate cost centres.

## Risk 6: safety incident

**Critical.**

Particularly with minors.

### Mitigation

Maintain strong safety protocols, documentation, trained personnel and appropriate insurance.

## Risk 7: software distraction

**High.**

Building software too early can consume attention before the operating model is proven.

### Mitigation

Use software internally first.

7. Do not build software before the internal operation has enough complexity and data.
8. Do not allow the experiences business to become disconnected from the equipment business.
9. Do not let retail inventory consume capital needed for high-utilization rentals.
10. Do not treat all tents as interchangeable.

# 75. Final recommendation

Camping Nigeria should not pivot away from its current programmes.

It should use them as the **demand engine** for a much larger equipment and operations business.

The strategic sequence I recommend is:

### Phase 1
**Best camping rental company in Nigeria**

Make renting easy, reliable and professional.

### Phase 2
**Best specialist supplier**

Sell only products Camping Nigeria trusts and understands.

### Phase 3
**Institutional infrastructure partner**

Schools, companies, NGOs, field teams and outdoor operators.

### Phase 4
**Distributor and private-label owner**

Use demand data to negotiate stronger manufacturing relationships.

### Phase 5
**Operating platform**

Build and commercialize the software only after Camping Nigeria has proven the workflow itself.

The central idea is:

> **Do not build a camping store. Build the infrastructure layer behind camping in Nigeria.**

If Camping Nigeria owns the equipment, supply chain, servicing, programme relationships, institutional distribution and operating data, the software becomes a natural extension of the business rather than a speculative pivot.

That is the path I would pursue.

1. schools;
2. corporate/organizational buyers;
3. individuals;
4. NGOs/field teams;
5. other outdoor and rental operators.

The goal should be to identify **what they repeatedly need to rent or buy**, not merely what products look attractive in China.
