# What We'll Be Sourcing in China for Camping Nigeria

The China trip should not be treated as a shopping trip. The goal is to build a supplier architecture.

> **How to read this document.** Supplier groups A–F are the core sourcing list; group G (apparel) is optional. The supplier groups, factory questions and Nigeria design notes are the original plan. Each **What to specify** table, each *Good answer* line under a factory question, and each **Spec** line in the Nigeria section is added guidance. All figures are starting targets for comparing factories and judging samples, not verified market prices. Confirm every one against a physical sample and a test report.

## Material rules of thumb

These decisions come up for almost every product group, so settle them once.

1. **Polyester over nylon for anything that sits in the sun.** Nylon stretches when wet and loses strength faster under UV. Polyester holds its pitch in a downpour and lasts longer in Nigerian sun. Keep nylon for packed-weight items like backpacking tents, if we ever carry them.
2. **Watch for PU hydrolysis.** Standard polyurethane coatings break down in heat and humidity. The tent goes sticky, flakes and smells, usually after two or three years in storage. Ask for hydrolysis-resistant (polyether-based) PU or a TPU laminate, and keep stock dry and ventilated.
3. **Ask for anti-mildew treatment** on all fabric. Wet returns will happen.
4. **Ask for UV-stabilised fabric and webbing** (UPF 50+ on canopies and flies). UV is what fails first on gear that is pitched outdoors for days at a time.
5. **Avoid fibreglass poles on anything we rent.** They splinter and shatter, and a splintered pole cuts hands. Aluminium or steel only.
6. **Hygiene items are for sale, not rent.** Pillows, hydration bladders and sleeping-bag liners are hard to sanitise between customers. Rent the shell product and sell the liner.
7. **Standardise components across models.** Pole sections, pegs, zip sliders and buckles shared between products mean fewer spare-part SKUs and faster repairs.
8. **Tag everything for the rental fleet.** Sewn-in serial or QR labels on every tent, bag and mat, so returns can be checked against what went out.

## Supplier groups

### Supplier group A: tents and shelters

Look for:

1. recreational tents;
2. family tents;
3. institutional group tents;
4. gazebos;
5. tarps;
6. replacement poles;
7. rainflies;
8. groundsheets.

**What to specify**

| Item | Materials | Specs to ask for | Design notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recreational tents (2–4 person) | 75D–150D polyester fly; polyester mesh inner; 150D–210D Oxford floor | Fly ≥ 3,000 mm hydrostatic head; floor ≥ 5,000 mm; all seams heat-taped; 7001-T6 or 6061 aluminium poles, 8.5–9.5 mm | Double-wall, with an all-mesh inner so the fly can come off on dry nights. Fly-first or simultaneous pitch, so the inner stays dry in rain. Two doors for cross-ventilation. |
| Family tents (4–8 person) | 150D polyester Oxford fly; 210D Oxford floor | Fly ≥ 3,000 mm; floor ≥ 5,000 mm; 11 mm aluminium or steel frame; YKK #8 or #10 zips on doors | Cabin or tunnel shape for standing headroom. Large vestibule for shade and bags. High and low vents on opposite sides. |
| Institutional group tents (8+ person) | 210D–300D polyester Oxford, or polycotton canvas; PE or 300D Oxford floor | Steel or heavy aluminium frame; flame-retardant fabric to CPAI-84; reinforced pegging points | Colour-coded poles and sleeves; few loose parts; one standard pole length where the design allows. **Consider polycotton** for fixed-site and school use: it is noticeably cooler and has less condensation, but it is heavy and must be fully dried before storage. |
| Gazebos (3 × 3 m) | Powder-coated steel hex frame, legs 30 mm or more (40 mm aluminium for premium); 420D–600D polyester PU canopy | UPF 50+; flame-retardant to CPAI-84 for events and schools; optional zip-on sidewalls | Must come with leg weights or sandbags and guy points. Squall winds at the start of the rainy season will lift an unanchored gazebo. |
| Tarps | 210D polyester Oxford ripstop with PU coating, or heavy PE (150–200 gsm) for budget | ≥ 2,000 mm; reinforced corners; grommets or webbing loops about every 50 cm | Standard 3 × 4 m size. Webbing loops outlast metal grommets. |
| Replacement poles | Same alloy and diameter as the tent's original | Shock cord included; ferrule spec matched | Buy pole **sections**, not only whole poles. One broken section should not scrap a full pole. |
| Rainflies | Same fabric as the tent's original fly | Same HH and taping as the original | Full coverage to near-ground. Light or silver-coated underside to reflect heat (see Heat below). |
| Groundsheets | 210D Oxford PU or heavy PE | ≥ 5,000 mm | Cut slightly smaller than the tent floor, so rain does not run between the layers. For rental, the groundsheet is the **sacrificial layer**: replacing one is far cheaper than replacing a floor. |

All lighting and power products need **CE and RoHS** marking, and test reports to support the **SONCAP** certificate needed to import into Nigeria.

### Supplier group G (optional): outdoor apparel and branded products

The China trip can explore:

- high-quality shirts;
- lightweight vests;
- hats;
- rain ponchos;
- socks;
- lightweight shoes.

But apparel should support the brand, not distract from the infrastructure business.

**Recommended first products**

- technical T-shirt;
- cap;
- rain poncho;
- lightweight utility vest.

Avoid large footwear inventory until demand is proven.

**Before you approach suppliers**

- **Apparel comes from garment factories, not tent factories.** Treat it as a separate supplier, and don't let a tent factory's trading arm add it to the order at a markup.
- **MOQs are usually per style, per colour,** then split across sizes. Agree the size split up front, and include **children's sizes** for school programmes.
- **Print method depends on fabric colour.** Sublimation is cheap and never cracks or peels, but it only works on white or light polyester. Dark garments need screen print or heat transfer. Embroidery suits caps and vests.

**What to specify**

| Item | Materials | Specs to ask for | Design notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Technical T-shirt** (first order) | 100% polyester or polyester-spandex knit, 120–160 gsm | Moisture-wicking and quick-dry; UPF 30+; anti-odour treatment; colourfastness to washing and sweat; pilling resistance | Light colours run cooler in sun. Flatlock seams so nothing rubs under a backpack. Logo placement that a backpack strap won't cover. |
| **Cap** (first order) | Quick-dry polyester or nylon front; mesh back panels | Wicking sweatband; adjustable strap | Embroidered logo. Mesh back for heat. For school groups, also price a wide-brim bucket hat with a neck flap, which gives far more sun protection. |
| **Rain poncho** (first order) | 190T–210T polyester or nylon with PU coating | ≥ 2,000 mm; taped seams; hood with drawcord | Reusable, not single-use. Long enough to cover a backpack. ## Factory questions

### Corporate

1. Are you the actual manufacturer?
   - *Good answer:* the business licence lists manufacturing in its scope, and they will walk you onto the production floor. Look for heat-taping machines and their own sewing lines. Trading companies are not automatically bad, but you should know which one you are dealing with.
2. Where is the factory?
3. What percentage is exported?
   - *Good answer:* more than half. Export factories already work to test reports and foreign standards.
4. Which countries?
5. Which outdoor brands do you manufacture for?
   - *Good answer:* named brands, even under NDA. Ask which of those brands' QC procedures they now use as standard.
6. Can references be verified?
7. Can Camping Nigeria audit production?
   - *Good answer:* yes, and they accept a third-party pre-shipment inspection (SGS, QIMA, V-Trust or similar) at AQL 2.5 for major defects and 4.0 for minor.

### Quality

8. What incoming material tests are performed?
   - *Good answer:* hydrostatic head, tear and tensile strength, UV resistance and colourfastness on each fabric lot. Ask for third-party test reports less than 12 months old.
9. What finished-product inspections?
   - *Good answer:* a pitch test on a sample from every batch, plus a seam and zip check.
10. What is the defect rate?
    - *Good answer:* a specific figure (typically 2–3% or less) and an explanation of how it is measured. "Very low" is not an answer.
11. How are waterproof ratings verified?
    - *Good answer:* in-house hydrostatic testing to ISO 811, with a machine you can see. Ask whether the rating is measured on new fabric only or also after abrasion or washing.
12. What seam-sealing process is used?
    - *Good answer:* heat-sealed tape on every fly and floor seam, with the tape width stated.
13. What pole materials are available?
    - *Good answer:* 7001-T6 and 6061 aluminium in several diameters, with steel for large frames. DAC poles as a premium option.
14. What zipper brand and specification?
    - *Good answer:* YKK or SBS, #8 or #10 coil on doors. Unbranded zips are the most common early failure.
15. What fabric coating?
    - *Good answer:* PU with a stated mm rating, and a hydrolysis-resistant option (see Material rules of thumb). Silicone-coated fabric cannot be seam-taped, so be wary of it on budget tents.
16. Can materials change without customer approval?
    - *Good answer:* **no**, written into the purchase order. Sign off a bill of materials and keep a sealed "golden sample" that production must match.

### Parts

17. Can poles be ordered separately?
18. Replacement rainflies?
19. Zippers?
20. Clips?
21. Buckles?
22. Storage bags?

This is critical for rental economics.

- *Good answer to 17–22:* a spare-parts price list with its own (low) MOQ, and a written commitment to keep those parts available for at least three years. As a starting point, order spares worth 5–10% of the unit count with the first order.

### Commercial

23. Sample MOQ?
    - *Good answer:* one to three pieces. Samples are usually paid for, with the cost refunded against the first order.
24. Private-label MOQ?
25. Price at 50 / 100 / 500 / 1,000 units?
26. Lead time?
    - *Typical:* 30–60 days after sample approval. Factories close for about four weeks around Chinese New Year (January or February), so avoid orders that straddle it.
27. Packaging dimensions?
28. Units per container?
    - *For planning:* a 20 ft container loads roughly 25–28 m³ and a 40 ft high-cube roughly 65–68 m³. Get carton dimensions (CBM) for every product so you can plan mixed loads.
29. FOB port?
    - *Ask:* which ports have direct or reliable sailings to Lagos (Apapa, Tin Can or Lekki Deep Sea Port). Ningbo, Shanghai, Xiamen and Shenzhen are the usual export ports.
30. Payment terms?
    - *Standard:* 30% deposit, with the 70% balance paid only after the pre-shipment inspection passes.

**Shipping and import (added)**

- **SONCAP.** Regulated goods entering Nigeria need SONCAP certification. Ask each factory to supply the test reports and documents the certification body will want, and check with the clearing agent before ordering.
- **Lithium batteries** (lanterns, headlamps, power stations) ship as dangerous goods (Class 9). The factory must provide a UN38.3 test report and an MSDS, and not every freight forwarder will carry them. Confirm this before you commit to any lighting or power supplier.

### Customization

31. Colour?
    - *Note:* a custom-dyed fabric colour usually carries a high MOQ (often thousands of metres). Choosing from the factory's stock colours is far cheaper.
32. Logo?
    - *Options:* screen print on the fly or canopy, woven labels, or reflective print. MOQs are usually low.
33. Custom carry bags?
    - *Ask for:* oversized bags. Rental customers never pack a tent back as tightly as the factory does.
34. Nigeria-specific ventilation?
    - *Ask for:* extra mesh panels, high and low vents, and an all-mesh inner. See Heat below.
35. Stronger floors?
    - *Ask for:* 210D Oxford at 5,000 mm or more, or a PE floor for institutional tents.
36. Replaceable pole systems?
    - *Ask for:* the same pole section lengths shared across models.
37. Heavier-duty zips?
    - *Ask for:* #10 on main doors.

## Nigeria-specific tent design

Camping Nigeria should not simply copy European camping specifications. Nigeria creates different requirements.

### Heat

Ventilation matters.

Prioritize:

1. large mesh panels;
2. cross ventilation;
3. shaded vestibules;
4. fly designs that reduce heat buildup.

**Spec:** an all-mesh inner tent with doors on opposite sides. Vents high on the fly to let hot air out, with low openings to let cool air in. A gap of several centimetres between fly and inner. A light-coloured or silver-coated fly to reflect sun: mid-tones such as sand, tan or grey reflect heat without showing dust the way white does. Mesh fine enough to keep out mosquitoes, but not ultra-fine "no-see-um" mesh, which cuts airflow noticeably. Compare mesh samples side by side.

### Rain

Heavy rainfall makes the following important:

1. seam sealing;
2. hydrostatic resistance;
3. bathtub floor;
4. drainage;
5. rainfly coverage.

**Spec:** fly at 3,000 mm or more and floor at 5,000 mm or more, every seam heat-taped. Bathtub floor walls 10–15 cm high, with corner seams away from the ground. Fly reaching close to the ground on all sides, with vents that have rain hoods. Fly-first or simultaneous pitching, so the inner is never exposed while setting up in rain.

### Dust

Products should be:

1. easy to wipe;
2. easy to clean;
3. tolerant of repeated deployment.

**Spec:** smooth coated fabrics rather than brushed or textured ones. Dark, wipe-clean floors. Cleanability is also a harmattan question: dust gets into zips, so spare sliders matter (see Repair products).

### Institutional use

School tents need:

1. simple setup;
2. fewer loose parts;
3. durable poles;
4. standardized components;
5. colour coding;
6. replaceable pieces.

**Spec:** colour-matched pole tips and sleeves or clips, so children can see which pole goes where. Poles attached to the tent body, or kept in one bag per tent. Flame-retardant fabric to CPAI-84. A setup diagram printed inside the carry bag. One standard pole and peg set across all school tents.

### Rental

Products need to survive:

1. inexperienced customers;
2. repeated folding;
3. wet returns;
4. transport;
5. rough handling.

The best rental tent may be heavier than the best personal backpacking tent.

**Spec:** 150D–210D fabrics rather than 68D–75D. Aluminium or steel poles, never fibreglass. #10 door zips. Hydrolysis-resistant PU and anti-mildew treatment, because wet returns are guaranteed. Oversized carry bags. A sacrificial groundsheet with every tent. A sewn-in serial or QR label. A spare-parts stock ordered with the first batch.
